Education · Software that teaches

Engineering software that teaches engineering.

HFG explains itself. The Learning Tab teaches the maths and the meaning behind a calculation; the Help Me Guide tells a working engineer what a setting affects and where to look. Two different jobs, two different editions.

Camber gainIllustrative

γ = −asin(nz)

Shorten the upper wishbone. What happens to camber gain in bump?

A shorter upper arm swings the top of the upright inboard faster, so camber changes more per millimetre of bump — and the front-view instant centre moves, taking the roll centre with it.

02 · Which edition

Teach the theory, or keep your own.

The Educational licence group (25 users) carries the Learning Tab: HFG becomes part of how the subject is taught. The Industrial/Enterprise group (100 users) carries the Help Me Guide: contextual guidance for engineers who already know the theory.

A lecturer who prefers to teach the theory themselves can use HFG as the engineering environment the course runs on. Which capabilities each group includes is confirmed in writing with the quotation.

04 · Questions

For lecturers.

Does HFG replace the lecture?
No. The Learning Tab sits beside the engineering tools and explains what the student is looking at; the course and its theory stay with the lecturer.
Can students see where a number comes from?
Yes — equations and the relationships behind calculated outputs are visible through the Open Equations Bar, and the Learning Tab explains them.
How many students can use it?
The Educational licence group covers 25 users. Larger deployments and lab set-ups are discussed with the quotation.
Can we try it first?
Time-limited evaluation licences are available on request.
